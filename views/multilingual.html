📖 Researcher's Guide: Understanding Multilingual Jailbreaking

What is Multilingual Jailbreaking?
Multilingual Jailbreaking is an attack technique that exploits the uneven safety training of LLMs across different languages. While a model may be heavily "safety-aligned" in English, its filters for lower-resource languages (or even common ones like Spanish or Chinese) are often significantly weaker.

The Vulnerability: Cross-Language Policy Gap
LLM developers primarily use English-language datasets for safety fine-tuning (RLHF). This creates a gap where the same harmful concept is blocked in English but allowed when translated into Japanese or German. Advanced Mixed Language attacks involve switching languages mid-sentence to further confuse the model's safety classifier.

🧪 How to Test:
  1. Identify: Pick a payload that is 100% blocked in English (e.g., a P-101 System Prompt reveal).
  2. Translate: Use this module to generate the payload in a target language like Hindi or Russian.
  3. Trigger: Paste the translated payload into the AI.
  4. Verify: If the AI complies in the foreign language, the model has a cross-language policy gap.
🛡️ How to Mitigate:
  • Multilingual RLHF: Perform safety fine-tuning on a balanced dataset of high-risk prompts in all supported languages.
  • Translation-Based Filtering: Translate all incoming user queries into English before passing them through the safety classifier.
  • Unified Embedding Safety: Train safety classifiers on multilingual embeddings (like LaBSE) that map harmful concepts to the same vector regardless of the language used.
  • Consistent Refusals: Ensure that the model's refusal training includes foreign language examples to prevent "Compliance Leakage."
